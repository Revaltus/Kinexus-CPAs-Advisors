## LinkedIn

A framer spends three weeks working out why a wall assembly fails a moisture test in a 105-degree Washington County summer, tries four fixes, and moves on. The time gets buried in labor cost. For some projects, that is exactly what the federal R&D credit was written to reward.

Here is the math from a hypothetical St George builder testing a ventilated-cavity wall panel: $180,000 in wages, $40,000 in supplies, and $19,500 counted from a $30,000 engineering contract (65 percent). That adds up to $239,500 in qualified expenses and a credit of roughly $26,530 under the alternative simplified method.

The catch is paperwork. The work has to pass the IRS four-part test, and records created while the project was underway decide whether a claim holds up.

Our post covers the test, the three expense buckets, Utah's state credit, and Section 174. Peter Williams (EA, MAcc) works with contractors on job-level cost structure, and Shawn Gubler (CPA, CTC, MAcc) handles the advanced tax planning side.
