A framing contractor in St. George hires eight carpenters and spends a year developing a prefabricated wall-panel system designed to withstand the challenges of 110-degree job sites. She tests adhesives, modifies the panel design three times, and works through several technical problems before finding a workable solution. Despite the investment, her company finishes the year with a tax loss.

Her bookkeeper tells her the research and development (R&D) tax credit is unavailable because the business owes no federal income tax.

That advice may be costing her money.

Under federal tax law, certain qualified small businesses can elect to apply a portion of their federal R&D tax credit against the employer's share of Social Security and Medicare payroll taxes. This provision can benefit qualifying startup businesses that are investing in research and development but have little or no income tax liability.

For construction companies, this creates an opportunity to recover some of the costs associated with developing innovative building methods, materials, products, and processes, even during an unprofitable year.

Here's what contractors need to know about eligibility, qualifying research activities, potential savings, documentation, and the steps involved in claiming the credit.

## Why a Business Can Claim the R&D Credit Even With a Tax Loss

The federal R&D tax credit, established under Internal Revenue Code Section 41, traditionally helps businesses reduce their federal income tax liability.

However, a business with little or no income tax liability may not be able to use the credit immediately. Unused research credits generally can be carried forward for up to 20 years.

Congress created an additional opportunity for qualifying small businesses through the payroll tax credit election. This allows eligible businesses to apply a portion of their research credit against certain employer payroll tax obligations instead of waiting until they generate taxable income.

The election is particularly valuable for young construction companies investing in new technologies, materials, engineering solutions, and building processes.

A contractor may have substantial payroll expenses while operating at a loss because of equipment financing, startup costs, bonding expenses, retainage, or investments in developing new construction methods.

The payroll tax election provides a way to potentially turn qualifying research expenditures into a more immediate financial benefit.

**The important distinction:** A business does not have to be profitable to qualify for the payroll tax election. However, it must satisfy the IRS definition of a qualified small business and conduct activities that meet the requirements for qualified research.

The payroll credit reduces eligible employer payroll tax obligations. It is not an unrestricted cash refund, and it cannot eliminate payroll taxes that belong to employees.

## Who Qualifies as a Qualified Small Business?

The IRS establishes two primary requirements for a business to qualify for the R&D payroll tax election.

**1. Less than $5 million in gross receipts**

The business must have less than $5 million in gross receipts for the tax year in which it is claiming the credit.

**2. A qualifying business history**

The business cannot have had gross receipts in any tax year before the five-tax-year period ending with the current tax year.

For example, a contractor whose first gross receipts occurred in 2022 may qualify for the 2025 tax year, assuming the other requirements are satisfied.

A contractor that first generated gross receipts in 2019 would not qualify for the 2025 election because receipts occurred before the applicable five-year period.

There are additional considerations for businesses operating through multiple entities. Related businesses, predecessors, and entities under common control may need to aggregate their gross receipts when determining eligibility.

The rules also account for the business's legal and tax structure. Corporations, S corporations, partnerships, sole proprietors, and certain other taxpayers may qualify when the applicable requirements are satisfied. Tax-exempt organizations under Section 501 are excluded.

**How long can a business use the election?**

A qualified small business can make the payroll tax election for up to five tax years. This is a cumulative limit, not a new five-year period every time a business makes an election.

The IRS will not permit an election for a tax year if the business has already made the election for five or more preceding tax years.

The business must independently satisfy the eligibility requirements for each tax year.

## How Much Can Contractors Offset Through the Payroll Tax Credit?

For tax years beginning after December 31, 2022, a qualified small business can elect to apply up to $500,000 of its federal R&D credit against eligible employer payroll taxes.

The credit is applied in the following order:

- **Employer Social Security tax:** The credit first offsets the employer's 6.2% share of Social Security tax, subject to a maximum offset of $250,000 per calendar quarter.
- **Employer Medicare tax:** Any remaining eligible credit can then offset the employer's 1.45% share of Medicare tax for that quarter.

There is no separate $250,000 annual Medicare allocation. The $500,000 limit is the maximum amount that can be elected for the tax year, while the $250,000 Social Security limitation applies separately to each quarter.

The credit cannot be applied against the employee's share of Social Security or Medicare taxes, federal income tax withholding, or the additional 0.9% Medicare tax withheld from certain higher-paid employees.

The actual benefit depends on the qualified research credit generated, the amount elected, and the employer payroll tax liabilities available to absorb the credit.

Any unused payroll tax credit carries forward to subsequent quarters.

## A Practical Example for a St. George Design-Build Contractor

Consider a hypothetical design-build contractor in Washington County, Utah.

The company first generated gross receipts in 2022. During 2025, it generated $2.8 million in revenue but reported a $60,000 tax loss.

The company paid $900,000 in W-2 wages during the year. Assuming all employees remained below the applicable 2025 Social Security wage base, its estimated annual employer payroll taxes were:

- **Employer Social Security tax:** $55,800
- **Employer Medicare tax:** $13,050
- **Total employer Social Security and Medicare taxes:** $68,850

During the year, a project manager, lead carpenter, and estimator devoted portions of their time to developing and testing a prefabricated wall-panel system.

After reviewing the activities, employee wages, qualifying supplies, and supporting documentation, the company determines that it has $360,000 in qualified research expenses.

Assume the company had no qualified research expenses in any of the three preceding tax years and qualifies to use the Alternative Simplified Credit (ASC) method.

Under the ASC method, when a business has no qualified research expenses in any one of the three preceding tax years, the credit calculation generally uses 6% of current-year qualified research expenses.

The calculation would be:

- **Qualified research expenses:** $360,000
- **Applicable credit percentage:** 6%
- **Estimated federal R&D tax credit:** $21,600

This calculation assumes the company does not elect the reduced credit under Section 280C. Instead, it must reduce its otherwise deductible or capitalized domestic research expenses by the applicable credit amount under Section 174A.

Alternatively, the company could elect the reduced credit under Section 280C. That would reduce the $21,600 credit to $17,064 (79% of the calculated credit), without the corresponding Section 174A deduction reduction required when the reduced-credit election is not made.

The company files its original 2025 federal income tax return in March 2026 and makes the payroll tax credit election on Form 6765.

Because the return was filed before April 1, the first eligible payroll tax quarter is Q2 2026.

For illustration, assume employer payroll taxes are evenly distributed throughout the year:

| Quarter | Employer Social Security | Employer Medicare | Credit applied |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q2 2026 | $13,950 | $3,262.50 | $17,212.50 |
| Q3 2026 | $13,950 | $3,262.50 | $4,387.50 |
| Total | $27,900 | $6,525 | $21,600 |

The credit first eliminates the Q2 employer Social Security liability and then the employer Medicare liability. The remaining $4,387.50 reduces the Q3 employer Social Security liability.

The result is $21,600 in reduced employer payroll tax obligations, even though the business reported a tax loss for 2025.

This example is illustrative. Actual credit calculations depend on the research activities, qualified expenses, tax-credit methodology, Section 280C election, payroll records, and available employer payroll tax liabilities.

## What Construction Activities Can Qualify for the R&D Tax Credit?

Construction companies often overlook research and development opportunities because they associate R&D with laboratories, scientists, and manufacturing facilities.

However, the IRS does not limit the research credit to a particular industry.

A construction business may qualify when it develops or improves a product, process, technique, or other business component and meets the IRS's four-part test.

The four requirements are:

- **Permitted purpose:** The research must relate to developing a new or improved business component, such as a product, process, technique, or formula, with the objective of improving its functionality, performance, quality, or reliability.
- **Technological in nature:** The research must rely on principles of engineering, physics, chemistry, biology, or computer science.
- **Elimination of uncertainty:** The business must encounter technological uncertainty regarding the capability, method, or appropriate design of the component.
- **Process of experimentation:** The business must evaluate alternatives, conduct testing, analyze results, or otherwise use a systematic process to resolve the technical uncertainty.

The four-part test must be applied separately to each business component being considered for the credit.

Here are examples of construction activities that may qualify:

| Construction activity | Potential eligibility | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Developing a new prefabricated or modular assembly method | Often yes | May involve technical uncertainty, prototype development, and repeated testing |
| Testing concrete mixes, adhesives, sealants, or stucco systems for extreme heat and UV exposure | Often yes | May involve experimentation to establish material performance |
| Developing a new structural design or engineering solution for challenging soil conditions | Potentially | Must involve unresolved technical uncertainty and experimentation |
| Developing a new construction sequencing or installation process to overcome an engineering challenge | Potentially | Must go beyond routine scheduling or project management |
| Developing new BIM-related software or technical automation tools | Potentially | Must meet the applicable software and research requirements |
| Following established construction plans and specifications | Generally no | Routine construction does not establish qualified research |
| Making cosmetic or aesthetic changes to a building design | Generally no | Aesthetic improvements alone do not meet the research requirements |
| Performing routine quality control or standard material testing | Generally no | Routine testing is specifically excluded |

**Important research exclusions**

Even when a project involves innovation, certain activities are specifically excluded from the federal R&D credit.

These include:

- Research conducted after the beginning of commercial production.
- Adapting an existing product or process to a particular customer's requirements.
- Duplicating an existing product or process.
- Routine data collection, surveys, and studies.
- Certain research involving internal-use computer software that does not meet the additional requirements.
- Research conducted outside the United States and its territories.
- Research in the social sciences, arts, or humanities.
- Research funded by another person or governmental entity.

**An important consideration: Customer-funded research**

Contractors must also evaluate whether their research is funded by another party.

Research may be excluded if a customer or another party funds the work. The determination depends on the contractual arrangement, including who bears the financial risk if the research is unsuccessful and who retains substantial rights to the research results.

A fixed-price contract does not automatically qualify, nor does a cost-plus arrangement automatically disqualify the work. The actual contract terms and economic circumstances matter.

Contractors should have their agreements reviewed before including customer-related research expenses in an R&D credit calculation.

## What Expenses Can Be Included in the R&D Credit?

Identifying qualifying activities is only part of the process. Contractors must also identify the expenses directly associated with those activities.

Qualified research expenses generally fall into three primary categories, with certain computer rental or lease costs also potentially eligible.

**1. Employee wages**

Eligible wages may include compensation paid to employees who directly perform qualified research, directly supervise qualified research, or directly support qualified research.

For example, a carpenter building and testing a new prototype wall assembly may have qualifying wages attributable to that work.

A project manager who directly supervises the technical experimentation may also have qualifying wages.

However, general project management, administrative duties, estimating, and ordinary construction labor do not automatically qualify.

Only the portion of compensation attributable to qualified services should be included. The applicable tax definition of qualified wages must also be satisfied.

**2. Supplies and materials**

Certain tangible supplies used directly in qualified research may qualify.

For example, adhesives, insulation, concrete mixtures, framing materials, or other supplies consumed during prototype development and testing may be eligible.

Materials incorporated into a customer's completed building or used for routine construction generally do not qualify simply because they are part of an innovative project.

**3. Contract research expenses**

Certain amounts paid to outside contractors or engineering professionals for qualified research may qualify.

Generally, 65% of eligible contract research expenses can be included in the credit calculation. A higher percentage may apply to certain qualified research consortium payments.

The contractor must evaluate the agreement, financial risk, research rights, and location of the research activities.

## Documentation That Supports Your R&D Credit

The IRS requires businesses claiming the R&D credit to retain records that substantiate their qualified research activities and expenses.

Construction companies often already have many of these records through their project management, payroll, and job-costing systems. The challenge is connecting those records to the specific research activities being claimed.

Important records include:

- Project names and descriptions identifying the technical challenge being addressed.
- Project start and completion dates.
- Engineering drawings, sketches, design revisions, and prototypes.
- Testing procedures, results, and documentation of unsuccessful attempts.
- Employee payroll records and reasonable documentation of time spent performing qualified research.
- Invoices and receipts for research-related materials and supplies.
- Contracts and agreements identifying financial risk and ownership rights.
- Documentation explaining the technical uncertainty and how the company evaluated potential solutions.

A practical approach is to establish project codes in your job-costing system for experimental development activities.

This helps separate qualifying research expenses from ordinary construction costs and makes it easier to substantiate the credit when preparing the tax return.

Good bookkeeping is not a substitute for a qualified R&D study, but it can make identifying and supporting eligible expenses considerably easier.

## The Forms and Timing: How Contractors Claim the Credit

Claiming the payroll tax credit requires coordination between the business income tax return and quarterly payroll tax filings.

**Step 1: Calculate the research credit using Form 6765.**

The business calculates its federal R&D credit using IRS [Form 6765](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-6765), Credit for Increasing Research Activities.

The business must identify qualifying research expenses, determine the appropriate credit calculation method, and complete Section D if it intends to make the payroll tax election.

Partnerships and S corporations make the payroll tax election at the entity level. Other qualifying business structures generally must complete Form 3800 before completing Section D of Form 6765.

**Step 2: Make the payroll tax election on the original tax return.**

The payroll tax election must be made on a timely filed original income tax return, including extensions.

It cannot be made for the first time on an amended return.

The business specifies the portion of its research credit it wants to apply against payroll taxes, up to the $500,000 limit, subject to the other applicable credit limitations.

The election is subject to the five-tax-year limitation described earlier and generally cannot be revoked without IRS consent.

**Step 3: Begin applying the credit after filing the income tax return.**

The payroll tax credit becomes available beginning with the first calendar quarter that starts after the business files the income tax return containing the election.

For example:

- **Income tax return filed March 2026:** First eligible quarter is Q2 2026.
- **Income tax return filed October 2026:** First eligible quarter is Q1 2027.

The filing date, rather than the date the return is prepared or the tax year being claimed, determines when the payroll credit becomes available.

**Step 4: Claim the credit on Form 8974 and Form 941.**

The business completes IRS [Form 8974](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8974), Qualified Small Business Payroll Tax Credit for Increasing Research Activities, and attaches it to its quarterly Form 941.

Form 8974 calculates the amount available to offset the employer's Social Security and Medicare taxes for the quarter.

The credit first reduces employer Social Security tax, subject to the $250,000 quarterly limit, and then reduces employer Medicare tax.

If the credit cannot be fully used, the remaining balance generally carries forward to subsequent quarters.

The business must continue following the applicable Form 8974 and Form 941 instructions when reporting the credit.

## Another Important Tax Change: Section 174A and Domestic Research Expenses

The federal tax treatment of research and experimental expenses also changed under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, enacted July 4, 2025.

Previously, Section 174 generally required businesses to capitalize and amortize research and experimental expenses over five years for domestic research and 15 years for foreign research.

New Section 174A restores the ability to deduct qualifying domestic research and experimental expenses in the year they are incurred for tax years beginning after December 31, 2024.

Businesses may also elect to capitalize and amortize qualifying domestic research expenses over a period of at least 60 months, beginning with the month in which the taxpayer first realizes benefits from the expenditures.

Foreign research expenditures remain subject to the 15-year amortization requirement.

**What about research expenses from 2022 through 2024?**

The legislation also provides transition rules for previously capitalized domestic research expenses.

Businesses generally may elect to recover their remaining unamortized domestic research expense balances from the 2022 through 2024 tax years in either of two ways:

- Deduct the remaining balance in full during the first tax year beginning after December 31, 2024.
- Deduct the remaining balance ratably over the first two tax years beginning after December 31, 2024.

For calendar-year businesses, these options generally mean recovering the balance in 2025 or spreading the deduction across 2025 and 2026.

Certain eligible small businesses also had the opportunity to elect retroactive application of the new Section 174A treatment to qualifying prior tax years beginning after December 31, 2021, and before January 1, 2025.

The deadline for this retroactive election was July 6, 2026. That deadline has passed.

These changes are important because the income tax deduction for research expenses and the R&D tax credit are separate tax benefits with coordination requirements under Section 280C.

Businesses should evaluate their Section 174A deductions, research credit, and applicable Section 280C election together rather than treating them as unrelated tax decisions.

**An important distinction:** Not every expense deductible under Section 174A automatically qualifies for the Section 41 research credit. The research must independently satisfy the applicable qualified research requirements.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can a contractor with no taxable profit still claim the R&D tax credit?**
A: Yes. A qualified small business may elect to apply up to $500,000 of its federal R&D credit against eligible employer payroll taxes, even when it has no federal income tax liability. The business must satisfy the IRS gross-receipts and business-history requirements and conduct qualified research activities.

**Q: Which payroll taxes can the R&D credit offset?**
A: The credit first offsets the employer's 6.2% share of Social Security tax, subject to a $250,000-per-quarter limit. Any remaining eligible credit can then offset the employer's 1.45% share of Medicare tax. It cannot reduce employee payroll tax withholding or the additional 0.9% Medicare tax.

**Q: When can a contractor begin using the payroll tax credit?**
A: The credit becomes available in the first calendar quarter beginning after the business files its income tax return containing the Form 6765 payroll tax election. The business then claims the available credit using Form 8974 attached to its quarterly Form 941.

**Q: Does a construction company need a research department to qualify?**
A: No. The IRS does not require a dedicated research department or a particular job title. A contractor may qualify through engineering, material development, prototype testing, or improvements to construction processes, provided the activities meet the four-part research test.

**Q: Can routine construction improvements qualify for the R&D credit?**
A: Not necessarily. Routine construction work, standard quality-control testing, cosmetic changes, and ordinary modifications generally do not qualify. The business must demonstrate technical uncertainty and a process of experimentation intended to resolve that uncertainty.

**Q: Can a contractor claim the R&D credit for work performed on a customer's project?**
A: Potentially. However, the business must evaluate whether the research is funded by the customer or another party. The contractor's financial risk and rights to the research results are important considerations. Contract terms should be reviewed before including the associated expenses.

**Q: Does the R&D tax credit require a profitable business?**
A: No. A qualifying small business can use the payroll tax election even if it operates at a loss. However, it must generate an eligible research credit and have applicable employer payroll tax liabilities against which the credit can be applied.
