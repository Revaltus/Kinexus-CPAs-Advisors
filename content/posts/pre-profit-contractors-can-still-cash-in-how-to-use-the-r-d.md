A framing contractor in St George hires eight carpenters and spends a year figuring out how to prefab wall panels that hold up on 110-degree job sites. She tests adhesives, reworks the panel design three times, and still finishes the year with a loss. Her bookkeeper tells her the R&D credit is off the table because she owes no income tax. That advice is out of date. A qualified small business can elect to apply the credit against its payroll taxes, which means a loss-making year can still produce a real reduction in what you send the IRS each quarter.

This post covers who qualifies, how much you can offset, what counts as research in construction, and the exact forms and timing involved.

## Why a loss does not shut you out of the credit

The regular R&D credit reduces income tax. If you have none, the credit carries forward and may sit for years. Congress created a second route for young companies: elect to use part of the credit against the employer's share of payroll tax instead. The credit is claimed on the income tax return, but the cash benefit shows up on your quarterly Form 941.

For a contractor, that distinction matters. Construction startups often run lean in the first few years because of equipment financing, bonding costs, retainage tied up on open jobs, and owner pay that gets reinvested. Taxable income is low or negative, yet payroll is real and the Social Security bill arrives every quarter. The payroll election matches the credit to the tax you are actually paying.

## Who counts as a qualified small business

Two tests apply for the tax year you are electing:

- **Gross receipts under $5 million.** This is measured for the current tax year. Related entities under common control are generally aggregated, so a contractor with a separate equipment LLC or a sister company needs to add those receipts together.
- **Early in your revenue history.** You cannot have had gross receipts in any tax year before the five-year period that ends with the current year. A contractor whose first receipts came in 2021 can still qualify for 2025. One that billed its first job in 2019 cannot.

The business must also have qualified research expenses, which is the part contractors tend to underestimate. You can make the election for up to five tax years, so a company that qualifies for a few years in a row can repeat it each time.

## How much you can offset, and against what

The annual cap is $500,000, but it is split in two. Up to $250,000 applies against the employer's 6.2 percent share of Social Security tax. Since the Inflation Reduction Act, an additional $250,000 can apply against the employer's 1.45 percent share of Medicare tax, for tax years beginning after 2022.

Most small contractors will not come close to the cap. Offsetting $250,000 of Social Security tax would take roughly $4 million of annual W-2 wages. The real limit for most companies is the size of the credit itself, and then the quarterly payroll tax available to absorb it.

## A worked example for a St George design-build contractor

Here is a hypothetical, using round numbers. A design-build firm in Washington County had its first receipts in 2022. In 2025 it billed $2.8 million and posted a $60,000 loss. Total W-2 wages were $900,000, so employer Social Security tax runs about $55,800 a year, or roughly $13,950 a quarter.

During the year, a project manager, a lead carpenter, and an estimator spent a meaningful share of their time developing and testing a prefabricated panel system. After sorting out which wages and supplies qualify, the firm documents $360,000 of qualified research expenses. A company with no qualifying spending in the prior three years can use the alternative simplified method, which is 6 percent of current-year qualified spending. The credit is $21,600.

The firm files its 2025 return in March 2026 with the payroll election made on Form 6765. The credit starts working in the first quarter that begins after the return is filed, which is the quarter starting April 1. Here is how it plays out:

| Quarter | Employer Social Security tax | Credit applied | Credit remaining |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q2 2026 | $13,950 | $13,950 | $7,650 |
| Q3 2026 | $13,950 | $7,650 | $0 |

The Q2 Social Security liability on Form 941 drops to zero, and Q3 drops by $7,650. That is $21,600 of cash that stays in the business, from a year that showed a loss on paper. The figures are illustrative, and your own number depends on what your records support.

## Contractor activities that can qualify

Research credit qualification rests on a four-part test: a permitted purpose (a new or improved product, process, or technique), work that is technological in nature, an effort to eliminate real uncertainty about capability or method, and a process of experimentation. Construction fits this better than most owners assume. It also has a few exclusions that catch people out.

| Activity | Usually qualifies? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Developing a new prefab or modular assembly method and testing it against failure | Often yes | Technical uncertainty and iterative testing |
| Testing concrete mixes, stucco systems, sealants, or adhesives for high heat and UV exposure | Often yes | Materials testing with documented trials |
| Design-build process work that solves a structural or sequencing problem with no known answer | Sometimes | Depends on whether uncertainty and experimentation are documented |
| Building BIM workflows, estimating automation, or drone-based site tools | Sometimes | Software built for internal use faces a higher bar |
| Following plans and specs on a standard job | No | Routine work with no uncertainty |
| Cosmetic or style changes, or research done after the product is in commercial use | No | Statutory exclusions |

One exclusion deserves special attention. If a customer pays you to solve a problem and you do not bear the financial risk or keep rights to the results, the work may be treated as funded research and fall outside the credit. A contract for a fixed-price design-build job reads very differently from a cost-plus engineering arrangement, so we read the contract before counting any wages. For a deeper look at the four-part test, see our article on [whether your construction innovation qualifies for the R&D tax credit](/resources/could-your-construction-innovation-qualify-for-the-r-d-tax-c).

## Documentation that holds up

The credit is only as strong as the paper behind it. Contractors have an advantage here because jobs already generate records, but those records need to be pointed at the right questions.

The files worth keeping:

- Job or project names tied to each experimental effort, with start and end dates.
- Notes, sketches, test results, and failed attempts. Failures are evidence of experimentation.
- Time records or a reasonable allocation for each person who worked on qualified activities.
- Invoices for materials consumed in testing, kept separate from materials installed on a client's building.
- Contracts showing who bore the financial risk and who owns the result.

The cleanest approach is to set up a simple project code in your job costing system for experimental work. It costs nothing, and it turns a reconstruction exercise next March into a report you can pull. This is where bookkeeping and tax planning overlap, and it is one reason we like to see a contractor's job costing before the year ends, not after.

## The forms and timing, step by step

1. **Calculate the credit on Form 6765.** The payroll election is made in Section D of [Form 6765](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-6765), attached to a timely filed income tax return, including extensions. It cannot be made on an amended return, so the first filing has to include it.
2. **Specify the amount.** You elect a dollar amount up to the lesser of the credit and the $500,000 cap. Any credit you do not elect carries forward for use against income tax later.
3. **File Form 8974 each quarter.** Attach [Form 8974](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8974) to your Form 941 for every quarter you apply the credit. It calculates how much you can use that quarter, limited by your employer Social Security tax (and Medicare tax once the first $250,000 bucket is used).
4. **Start in the right quarter.** The credit applies beginning with the first quarter that starts after your income tax return is filed. File in March and Q2 is your first quarter. File on extension in October and the first benefit may not arrive until January.

That last point is why timing matters. A contractor who files early on a loss year can begin recovering cash six months sooner than one who waits until the extension deadline. The rules for deducting research costs also changed for 2025, so we model the income tax side alongside the credit, rather than treating the credit in isolation. ## Common Questions

**Can a contractor with no profit still claim the R&D credit?**

Yes, if it qualifies as a small business. With under $5 million in gross receipts and first receipts within the past five tax years, you can elect to apply up to $500,000 of credit against employer payroll taxes. Income tax liability is not required.

**Which payroll taxes can the credit offset?**

The employer share of Social Security tax (6.2 percent) first, up to $250,000. Under the Inflation Reduction Act, up to $250,000 more can apply to the employer share of Medicare tax (1.45 percent) for tax years beginning after 2022. The credit never reduces withheld employee taxes.

**When does the payroll offset start?**

It begins with the first quarter that starts after you file the income tax return containing the Form 6765 election. A return filed in March 2026 starts the credit with the quarter beginning April 1, 2026. You then claim it quarterly on Form 8974 attached to Form 941.
